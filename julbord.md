Cold dishes
Julskinka — Christmas ham: The classic Christmas ham, traditionally boiled or baked, then coated with mustard and breadcrumbs and browned.
Sill — pickled herring: A fundamental part of the julbord. Several varieties are traditionally served, including:
Senapssill — mustard herring: Pickled herring in a mustard-based sauce.
Löksill — onion herring: Pickled herring with onion.
Inlagd sill — basic pickled herring: Herring preserved in a sweet-sour vinegar brine.
Gravlax — cured salmon: Salmon cured with salt, sugar and dill.
Rökt lax — smoked salmon: Traditionally cold- or hot-smoked salmon.
Ägghalvor — egg halves: Boiled eggs, traditionally served with toppings such as mayonnaise, roe or shrimp.
Leverpastej — liver pâté: A smooth pâté traditionally made from liver and pork.
Sylta — head cheese/jellied meat: Cooked meat set in its own gelatin; an old-fashioned but genuinely traditional Christmas dish.
Rödbetssallad — beetroot salad: A traditional creamy beetroot salad, often containing apple.
Pressylta — pressed head cheese: A traditional cold meat preparation made from pork and set in gelatin.
Hot dishes
Janssons frestelse — Jansson's Temptation: A creamy casserole of potatoes, onions, cream and ansjovis — Swedish spiced sprats, not ordinary anchovies.
Köttbullar — Swedish meatballs: Small meatballs, traditionally served hot.
Prinskorv — “prince sausages”: Small Swedish sausages, usually fried before serving.
Julkorv — Christmas sausage: A traditional Swedish Christmas sausage, particularly associated with older regional traditions.
Revbensspjäll — Christmas ribs: Pork ribs, commonly roasted and sometimes glazed.
Lutfisk — lutefisk: Dried white fish treated with lye and then rehydrated and cooked. Traditionally served with potatoes and white sauce.
Dopp i grytan — “dip in the pot”: Bread dipped into the stock from the Christmas ham. A very old Christmas tradition.
Brunkål — brown cabbage: White cabbage cooked slowly with syrup, traditionally associated especially with southern Sweden.
Kokt potatis — boiled potatoes: A basic and important accompaniment to many julbord dishes.
Bread and accompaniments
Knäckebröd — crispbread: Traditional Swedish hard, dry bread, commonly served with butter.
Vörtbröd — Christmas malt bread: Soft, slightly sweet bread flavoured with malt and Christmas spices.
Kavring — dark rye bread: Dense, dark and slightly sweet Swedish rye bread.
Smör — butter: Served with the bread.
Senap — mustard: Particularly important with Christmas ham and sausages.
Julost — Christmas cheese: Cheese traditionally served with the Christmas meal; the exact type varies.
Traditional Christmas desserts
Risgrynsgröt — Christmas rice porridge: Rice cooked slowly in milk. Traditionally eaten around Christmas and sometimes served with cinnamon, sugar and milk.
Ris à la Malta: Cold rice pudding mixed with whipped cream, traditionally served as a Christmas dessert.
Mandel — the Christmas almond: Traditionally, one whole almond may be hidden in the rice porridge. The person who finds it is said to have good luck, traditionally sometimes associated with being married within the coming year.
Traditional Christmas sweets
Knäck: Traditional Swedish Christmas toffee made from sugar, syrup and cream, often with chopped almonds.
Kola — Swedish toffee: Chewy or firm caramel-like sweets, traditionally homemade for Christmas.
Ischoklad — “ice chocolate”: Small chocolate sweets made with chocolate and coconut fat; they melt very easily in the mouth.
Klenäter: Traditional deep-fried Christmas pastries, usually made from a sweet dough and coated with sugar.
Pepparkakor — Swedish gingerbread: Thin, crisp spiced gingerbread cookies, strongly associated with Swedish Christmas.
Nötter — nuts: Hazelnuts and walnuts are traditional Christmas snacks.
Traditional Christmas drinks
Julmust: A Swedish non-alcoholic Christmas soft drink with a dark colour and sweet, malty, spiced flavour.
Julöl — Christmas beer: Beer traditionally served with the Christmas meal.
Svagdricka: A traditional weak, lightly fermented Swedish malt drink.
Glögg: Swedish mulled wine, traditionally made with wine, sugar and Christmas spices. It may be alcoholic or alcohol-free.
Snaps — aquavit: Small shots of spirits, traditionally aquavit, served with the julbord. Drinking songs (snapsvisor) are traditionally associated with it.
Mumma: A traditional Swedish Christmas drink based on beer, with recipes varying between families and regions.
Kaffe — coffee: Traditionally served after the meal, particularly with Christmas sweets.
Traditional Christmas-season foods
These are strongly traditional Swedish Christmas foods, although they are not necessarily part of the julbord itself:
Lussekatter — saffron buns: Sweet saffron buns traditionally associated with Lucia, celebrated on 13 December.
Saffransbröd — saffron bread: Traditional saffron-flavoured Christmas bread.
Pepparkakor — gingerbread cookies: Traditionally eaten throughout Advent and Christmas.
Glögg: Traditionally served during Advent and Christmas, often with gingerbread and saffron buns.
